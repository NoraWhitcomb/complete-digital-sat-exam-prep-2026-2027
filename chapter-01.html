Question 1

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Tagged bees kept foraging under light cloud but foraged far less in rain): it states the main point. It sums up the whole study, which found that bees kept foraging in light cloud but made far fewer trips when it rained. The text never compares meadows with other places, and it never says why researchers tag bees in general. The text also does not say bees fly more on cloudy days than on sunny days, so that choice adds a comparison that is not there.

Question 2

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (Arjun feels nervous before his performance but gathers himself and goes on stage): it states the main point. Arjun's repeated finger flexing shows nerves, and his slow breath before walking out shows him gathering himself. Nothing in the text suggests he regrets his piece, and the nervous habits do not fit someone who is relaxed and confident. The text never mentions the stage lights being off, so the idea that he waits for them is invented.

Question 3

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (It began with forty gardening tools that were donated by people in the town): it matches what the text directly states. The text says the program started with forty tools donated by local residents. The two hundred tools came later, after the collection grew, so they do not describe how the program began. The repair workshops were also added later, and the text never says residents asked for them.

Question 4

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (Moss misted each morning grew faster than identical moss that was left dry): it most directly supports the claim. This finding compares misted moss with identical moss left dry and shows the misted moss grew faster. Saying misted moss grew at the same rate as unmisted moss would weaken the claim, not support it. The other two findings change the wrong thing, such as indoor versus outdoor moss or warm temperatures, so they say nothing about misting.

Question 5

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (Seed M had the greatest mass and took the most days of the four to sprout): the data match the claim. Seed M had both the greatest mass and the longest sprouting time, which matches the idea that heavier seeds take longer. The fact that two seeds sprouted in under ten days says nothing about mass, so it does not test the expectation. The other choices describe only one seed each, and a single seed cannot show a link between mass and time.

Question 6

Answer: A

The correct answer is A ("Whenever a neighbour needed help, he set down his work and spent the afternoon there."): it shows the claim. This quotation shows the uncle setting down his own work to spend an afternoon helping a neighbour. Opening a shop early shows he is busy, but it does not show him being generous with his time. Listening quietly at dinner and counting money each night show other traits, not generosity toward neighbours.

Question 7

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Tested timber panels have led some cities to approve much taller wooden buildings): it states the main point. The text explains that timber panels passed strict tests, which led several cities to approve wooden buildings over ten storeys tall. The text says nothing about cost, so the idea that wood is cheaper than concrete or steel is not supported. It also never says the glue makes panels lighter, and it says cities now allow tall wooden buildings rather than banning wood.

Question 8

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (The ice began forming later but still reached about the same greatest thickness): it matches what the text directly states. The researchers found that ice formed about nine days later, yet the thickest ice reached roughly the same depth as before. The text does not say the ice became thinner or melted earlier, so that choice adds details that are not there. Ice forming nine days earlier reverses the finding, and the text describes a clear pattern rather than no pattern.

Question 9

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (Port trade records show that most new jobs during that period came from shipping): it most directly weakens the claim. If most new jobs came from shipping, port trade, not fishing, would best explain the town's growth. Steady growth in the number of fishing boats matches the historian's own evidence, so it supports her argument instead of weakening it. An existing small port and fishing families living near the harbour do not show which industry drove the growth.

Question 10

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Riders used the route most in the early morning hours): it uses the data to match the claim exactly. The table shows 340 riders from 6:00 to 9:00, far more than at any other time. Comparing late afternoon with early afternoon says nothing about the early morning, which is the focus of the claim. The facts about fewer than 100 riders and about 120 riders are true but describe other times of day.

Question 11

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (X-ray images suggest Lin's early landscapes involved more careful work than critics thought): it states the main point. Grant points to X-ray images showing that Lin reworked each scene many times, which suggests more careful work than critics believed. Saying she painted quickly in a simple style repeats the critics' view, which the text argues against. The text does not rank X-ray imaging against other tools, and it never compares her early and later paintings.

Question 12

Answer: C

The correct answer is C ("At last the ice lets go its grip, and I can breathe the softened air."): it shows the claim. The ice letting go and the softened air show winter ending, and being able to breathe shows the speaker's relief. The deep snow and the frozen river describe winter still in place, so they show no relief that it is over. The line about wishing for winter's cold looks ahead to summer and does not express relief.

Question 13

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Breakfast eaters scored higher on a morning memory test than similar skippers): it most directly supports the claim. It compares breakfast eaters with similar students who skip breakfast and shows higher morning memory scores. Feeling happier in the afternoon is about mood, not about morning memory tasks. Similar scores for breakfast skippers would weaken the hypothesis, and a lunch study tests the wrong meal and time of day.

Nothing suggests she is embarrassed or that she wants to show the violin to other passengers.

Question 15

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (The puppy gained 4 kg by month 2, then only 1.5 kg more): the data match the claim. The puppy gained 4 kg in its first two months and only 1.5 kg after that. The final weight of 7.0 kg does not compare how fast the puppy gained weight in different months. Gaining weight each month, or weighing more at month 3 than at month 2, does not show that the first two months were fastest.

Question 16

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Wider roads may not ease traffic for long because they invite more driving): it states the main point. The text explains that traffic returned after roads were widened, likely because extra lanes encouraged more driving. The text says nothing about construction costs, and it does not compare large cities with small towns. Researchers in the text question building more lanes, so the idea that they recommend more lanes reverses their view.

Question 17

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Lizards measured on the rocky island had longer average leg length than forest lizards): it most directly supports the claim. Measuring average leg length on the island and in the forest directly tests whether island lizards have longer legs. Being active during the day and eating more kinds of insects describe behaviour, not leg length. Longer tails are a different body part, so that finding does not support a claim about legs.

Question 18

Answer: C

The correct answer is C ("She sold her only good coat to buy the books my school required."): it shows every part of the claim. Selling her only good coat to buy school books shows the grandmother giving up her own comfort for learning. Loving warm kitchens and telling stories about her village show other parts of her life, not a choice for learning. Wearing the same coat for years shows the family was poor, but it does not link that sacrifice to learning.

Question 20

The claim compares walking with cycling and the bus combined, and 46 is greater than 18 plus 21, or 39. Comparing the bus with cycling, or walking with the car, does not combine cycling and the bus. Saying the car was least popular is true but has nothing to do with the claim about walking.

Question 21

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (Sea otters help kelp forests survive by keeping the number of sea urchins down): it states the main point. The text shows that where otters vanished, urchins ate the kelp, and where otters returned, the kelp grew back. The text never says urchins are the only animals that eat kelp along the coast. It also does not say urchins moved to deeper water, and it presents otters as the cause of kelp regrowth, not the result.

Question 22

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (Mr. Hale: "Who sent you here, and why do you want to know where I live?"): it shows the claim. Hale demands to know who sent the visitor and why he wants to know where Hale lives, which shows suspicion of strangers. Inviting someone in from the rain shows a welcoming attitude, which is the opposite of suspicion. Living in one house for forty years and talking about muddy roads say nothing about how he treats strangers.

Question 23

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (Similar students who did not use the app improved their reading speed equally): it most directly weakens the claim. If similar students who did not use the app improved just as much, the app cannot be the reason for the gain. Students enjoying reading more and spending twenty minutes a day on the app do not show that the app caused faster reading. Students reading faster at the end of term repeats the study's own result, so it cannot weaken the claim.

Question 25

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Clay soil held 410 mL per kilogram, more than both sandy soil and loam): the data match the claim. Noting that sandy soil held the least, or adding all three totals, does not show that clay held the most.
