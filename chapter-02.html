Question 59

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (the park birds may sing higher so their songs can be heard over traffic): it follows from the text. Higher songs are easier to hear over low traffic noise, so park birds may sing higher to be heard. The text never mentions the birds' size, so the idea that forest birds are larger is unsupported. The text says nothing about how often the birds sing or about learning songs from another species.

Question 60

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (the villagers may have faced their houses south to gain warmth from the sun): it follows from the text. South-facing walls receive the most sun in a cold region, so the villagers likely faced their houses south for warmth. Nothing in the text links the direction of the houses to trade with the south. The text does not compare the ages of villages, and it describes the winters as long and cold, not warm.

Question 61

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (among low-composting areas, the one with workshops rose much more): it uses the data to match the claim exactly. Elm, which had workshops, rose from 12% to 31%, while Cedar rose only from 11% to 14%. Birch rose by only 6 points, so the workshops did not raise composting by over twenty points everywhere. Maple did not have the highest share after the change, and the areas without workshops did not start lower overall.

Question 62

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (It measures whether customers liked the softness, not how long they slept): it names the real problem with the evidence. The company asked whether customers liked the pillow's softness, which says nothing about how long they slept. Forty customers is not too many, and the survey did not measure sleep at all, so it cannot show how much longer people slept. The text never mentions a comparison with other pillows.

Question 63

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (He was pleased by something in the letter he had received that day): it follows from the text. Oskar hums all afternoon and smiles at nothing, which suggests something in the letter pleased him. His cheerful actions do not fit someone who is worried or confused. The text never mentions him planning a reply, so that choice adds an idea that is not there.

Question 64

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (the fish may use energy for protection instead of growth when predators are present): it follows from the text. Fish facing predators grew more slowly but had thicker scales, which suggests they used energy for protection instead of growth. The text gives no reason to think fish without predators would grow thicker scales. Nothing supports the idea that predators prefer slow fish with thick scales, or that moved fish would grow at the same rate.

Question 65

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Other safety changes were made at the same time and could also explain the drop): it follows from the text. The crossing guard and new crosswalk paint were added at the same time, so they could also explain the drop. The size of the speed limit change does not show what caused the fewer collisions. Collisions being common near schools is not mentioned, and the text says the paint was added during the same period, not later.

Question 66

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (The small group surveyed may not represent people across the whole province): it names the real problem with the evidence. 25 members of one school book club are unlikely to represent everyone in the province. The text does not say the survey left out e-books, so that weakness is invented. How much club members read, or what the survey found, does not fix the problem of a narrow sample.

Question 67

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (It is not supported, because Town P had more rain in June): it uses the data to match the claim exactly. Town P had 64 mm in June, more than Town Q's 58 mm, so Town Q did not get more rain in every month. Town Q's higher rain in July and August, or its higher total, ignores the word every in the claim. Saying Town P had more rain in July and August reads the table backward.

Question 68

The harbour went from crowded to empty after a new railway opened nearby, which suggests the railway drew trade away. The text describes the same harbour in both entries, so the writer did not move away. Nothing says the harbour was rebuilt, and the railway came after the crowding, not because of it.

Priya packs rain gear even though the sky is clear, and her frown suggests she expects rain later. Her frown does not fit someone hoping for rain on the way to school. Question 70

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (the beehives may help more apple blossoms get pollinated in Town M): it reflects how the two findings relate. Town M keeps beehives among its trees and harvests far more apples, so the hives may help more blossoms get pollinated. The text says the towns have similar weather and soil, which rules out richer soil as the reason. It never mentions how many trees each town plants or how much honey the bees make.

Question 71

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Libraries and bookshops may both be more common in larger towns): it names the real problem with the evidence. Larger towns may simply have more of both libraries and bookshops, so one does not have to cause the other. Libraries and bookshops offering different books does not explain the link between them. The size of individual libraries and the choice of towns over cities do not address the flaw in the reasoning.

Question 72

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (linked to the later snowmelt that delays growth at higher elevations): it follows logically from the details in the text. Snow melts later at high elevations, and the plant cannot grow until the ground is clear, so later snowmelt delays its flowering. The text never mentions insects or a separate species. The later flowering cannot cause later snowmelt, so that choice reverses cause and effect.

Question 73

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Visits rose from 45 in week 2 to 61 and then 74 afterward): the data match the claim. Visits rose from 45 in week 2 to 61 in week 3 and 74 in week 4, after the new seed was added in week 3. Visits staying above 40 in every week does not show a change after the new seed. The rise between weeks 1 and 2 happened before the change, and comparing only weeks 1 and 4 skips the timing of the change.

Question 74

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (He feels attached to the shop and finds it hard to leave it): it follows from the text. Okoro sweeps twice and stands in the doorway for a long time, which shows he is attached to the shop. Lingering in the doorway does not fit someone who is relieved to stop his chores. The text never mentions a new owner, and sweeping slowly on purpose is not forgetfulness.

Question 75

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (Spring floods may leave rich soil that helps that field grow more corn): it follows from the text. The field closest to the river floods each spring and gains a layer of rich soil, which can help corn grow. The text never mentions differences in sunlight or in the kind of corn planted. It also says nothing about the river keeping insects away.

Question 76

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (People who are already healthy may simply be more likely to visit the park): it names the real problem. People who are already healthy may be more likely to visit the park, so the park may not have caused their better health. The park's size and the nearby playgrounds do not affect whether visits caused better health. Saying visitors report better health simply repeats the evidence instead of describing its limit.

Question 77

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (the openings may have reduced the sway caused by fast winds): it follows logically from the details in the text. The only difference between the designs was the openings in the deck, and the design with openings barely moved. Both designs were made of the same material, so a stronger material cannot explain the difference. Both had the same length, and the text gives no reason to doubt what wind tunnels show.

Question 78

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (It is not supported: 60% said yes at North, but 30% at South): the data match the claim. 48 of 80 is 60% at North, while 120 of 400 is only 30% at South, so support was stronger at the smaller school. Counting more yes answers at South ignores that South surveyed far more students. Saying North surveyed fewer students is true but does not explain whether support was stronger.

Question 79

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (poor harvests may have driven the rise in flour prices): it follows logically from the details in the text. Letters describe dry fields and failed crops in the same year the flour price nearly doubled, which suggests poor harvests raised prices. The text never says the newspaper raised its own price. One year's rise does not show that prices doubled every year, and letters did not cause people to buy more flour.

Question 80

The second researcher found the same night and warm weather pattern but only near ponds with few fish, which adds a condition. The second finding does not say frogs call mostly during the day. Question 81

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (Students tested with calm music reported less stress than similar students without music): it most directly supports the claim. Comparing students tested with calm music to similar students without music, and finding less stress, directly supports the claim. Question 83

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (It is weakly supported, since Brand Z lasted only slightly longer): it uses the data to match the claim exactly. Brand Z lasted 321 minutes, only a few minutes more than the others, so the claim of much longer is only weakly supported. Having the highest number does not show it lasted much longer, and all four brands passing 300 minutes does not show that either. Brand W lasted 312 minutes, which is less than Brand Z, so that choice misreads the table.

Question 85

Question 86

Answer: D

The correct answer is D (It comes from a single person and does not compare against people without carrots): it names the real problem. Question 88

Answer: C

The correct answer is C (a planet may be orbiting the star about once every 12 days): it follows logically from the details in the text. Here, a planet passing in front of a star can block a little light, and the star dims regularly every 12 days. Stars that are burning out would not dim on a steady repeating schedule, and the text gives no sign of that. Clouds on Earth would not follow a 12-day pattern for years, and the text never mentions colour changes.

The graph shows no data after 2020, so it cannot show a fall either.

Question 91

Answer: A

The correct answer is A (a result of the grass strips keeping soil out of the river): it follows logically from the details in the text. The grass slows runoff so that soil settles before reaching the river, which keeps the water clearer. The clearer water came after the grass was planted, so it cannot be the reason farmers planted it. Heavier rain would carry more soil into the river, and the text links the change to the grass, not to chance.

Question 92

Answer: B

The correct answer is B (Randomly assign similar plants to receive fertilizer or no fertilizer, keeping other conditions the same): it most directly supports the claim. Random assignment of similar plants to fertilizer or no fertilizer, with everything else the same, isolates the fertilizer's effect. Giving fertilizer to every plant leaves nothing to compare against. Asking gardeners gives opinions, not measurements, and comparing sunny and shady gardens adds a second difference.
